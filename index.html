In clinical neuropsychological practice (e.g. WAIS-IV / Woodcock-Johnson IV), when large discrepancies occur across subtests (such as between high fluid reasoning and low processing speed), an overall FSIQ score is mathematically non-unitary. A full clinical diagnosis of giftedness, twice-exceptionality (2e), or cognitive impairment requires in-person proctored testing by a licensed clinical neuropsychologist.
